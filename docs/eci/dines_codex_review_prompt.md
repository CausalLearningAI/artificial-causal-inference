# Prompt for Codex: adversarial review of the DINES theory report

You are reviewing a mathematical theory report as an adversarial referee. Your job is to find errors, not to praise. Read the whole report first:

`docs/eci/dines_theory.md` (in this repository).

## Context you need

- **DINES** (Direct–Indirect Neural Effect Search) is a proposed successor to **NES** (Neural Effect Search; Mencattini, Cadei, Locatello, *Exploratory Causal Inference*, arXiv 2510.14073). The setting: a randomized binary treatment $T$; post-treatment images/videos $X$; a fixed dictionary of $m$ atoms $Z=\psi(X)$ (sparse-autoencoder codes). The goal is to find which atoms the treatment changes *directly*, and to file the other affected atoms under the direct atoms that carry their effect.
- The selection guarantee is adapted from **NEXIS** (Cadei et al., arXiv 2606.17010, §4 and Appendix A): forward IAMB-style search, then a terminal backward rule that keeps an atom only if it is significant at level $\alpha/m$ given every subset of the other selected atoms. NEXIS's precision proof bounds each false coordinate by its test at one *fixed* true null contained in the forward output, which avoids post-selection inference. The key difference here is that the dictionary is *post-treatment*, so the target set itself is not a separating set for all false atoms.
- The null hypothesis throughout is a **mean** null: $H_0(j\mid A):\ \mathbb{E}[Z^j\mid T,Z^A]=\mathbb{E}[Z^j\mid Z^A]$ a.s. The theory uses the exact null only; a relevance margin $\delta$ appears only as a practical option (§7.8).
- The report labels every result as Theorem / Proposition / Lemma / Corollary (claimed full proof), Conjecture, Open question, or Hypothesis (empirical). Appendix A lists them.

## What to check

1. **Every proof step.** For Lemmas 1–5, Theorem 6, Propositions 7, 8, 10, 13, 14, Theorem 11 and Corollary 12: is each step valid? Are the conditional-independence manipulations (weak union, contraction, decomposition, functions of conditioning variables, null sets in Lemma 2) used correctly? Is any step a sketch presented as a proof?
2. **Lemma 5 (separator inside the Markov blanket of a root).** This is the crux. Claim: if $T$ has no parents in a DAG and $s$ is not a child of $T$, then $D^\star(s)=\mathrm{MB}(T)\setminus(\{s\}\cup\mathrm{De}(s))$ d-separates $T$ from $s$. Try hard to break it: spouses that are descendants of $T$ through long chains, spouses that are also children, multiple children sharing co-parents, co-parents that are descendants of other children, $s$ outside the blanket. Check the moralization argument (ancestral set, moral edges only between parents of a common child inside the ancestral set). Check the remark that it fails for non-root targets.
3. **Theorem 6 and its assumptions.** Are A1 (latent SCM, measurement noise independent of $(T,L)$ but arbitrarily dependent across atoms), A2 (alignment on the Markov blanket of $T$: determination + purity) and A3 (atom-level mean adjacency faithfulness at the children) *sufficient*? Find a model that satisfies all three where $\mathrm{Adj}_Z(T)\neq J_C$. Pay attention to: shared measurement noise between principal and non-principal atoms; hidden variables outside the blanket; atoms that read hidden variables; principal atoms of children that are impure (purity is only *used* for spouse atoms and for the atoms of $D^\star$ — is that enough, or does Lemma 3 or Lemma 4 secretly need purity of child atoms too?).
4. **Necessity counterexamples (§4.3 (a)–(f)).** Check each computation (conditional means in the Gaussian models, the collider calculation, the cancellation case, the duplicate-atom case, near-determinism of $T$). Does each example really satisfy all assumptions except the one it targets? The report claims the brief's "no hidden common cause among affected factors" is needed only as "no hidden co-parent of a child", and that hidden common causes of two non-child affected factors are harmless. Try to find a counterexample.
5. **Theorem 11 (selection consistency; $\liminf\mathbb{P}(\hat S_n=\mathrm{Adj}_Z(T))\ge1-\alpha$).** Hunt for counterexamples to the **precision** part specifically. Is every false atom that can survive the terminal step really bounded by a test at a *fixed* conditioning set contained in the forward output? Is the favorable event in step (i) correctly built (finite intersection over fixed sets)? Does the forward phase's data-dependent path cause any hidden post-selection problem? Is the terminal level $\alpha/m$ enough without correcting for the exponentially many subset tests? Is Assumption P2 (mean forward completeness) stated in the form the proof needs? Construct, if you can, a distribution satisfying P and A5 where precision fails.
6. **Proposition 13 (indirect output).** Check the shrink-step consistency argument at a vanishing level $\alpha_n$ (in particular the claim that asymptotic validity at fixed levels implies $\mathbb{P}(p\le\alpha_n)\to0$), and the union bound giving $1-\alpha-\alpha_2$. Check Proposition 10's path argument under Assumption N1, and whether N1 + CF are the right conditions.
7. **Lemma 1 and the test section (§7).** Is the identity $\mathbb{E}[(T-\pi_A)(Z^j-\mu_{j,A})]=\mathbb{E}[\pi_A(1-\pi_A)\Delta_{j,A}]$ right? Is it correct that the GCM is valid under the *mean* null (not only under conditional independence) with the stated rate conditions? Is the binarized-stratification leakage formula right (small-effect limit $1-2/\pi$ of the retained fraction)? Is the cluster-robust statistic correct? Is the statement that permutation tests are exact only for the unconditional step correct?
8. **Proposition 8 (mechanism shift)** and **Proposition 14 (covariates)**: check the proofs, especially the modified ancestral set in Proposition 14.
9. **Section 8 (critique of NES).** The report makes specific claims about the NES paper and code: the target is undefined; Eq. 28 excludes mediation; the "argmax stays principal" argument after Eq. 26 is false (explicit counterexample $v_1=(3,0,-1)$, $v_2=(0,1.5,2)$, $\tau^Y=(1,1)$ — check that it satisfies Eqs. 25, 26, 31 and Assumption A.1); the transport error $\Delta_{j,t}(g)$ before Eq. 29 is identically zero in a randomized experiment; the "FWER → 0" claim is false at fixed $\alpha$; the residualization branch of the code is never executed by NES. If you have the NES paper (arXiv 2510.14073) or code available, verify the equation references and the quoted claims; otherwise check internal consistency. Flag any statement that is unfair, overstated, or not supported by the evidence given.
10. **Labels and honesty.** Is anything labelled Theorem/Proposition that is actually conjectural? Is any "measured" number used to support a claim it cannot support? Are any assumptions stated as weaker than they are?

## How to report

Return a numbered list of findings. For each finding give:
- **Severity**: `critical` (a stated theorem/proposition is false or its proof has a gap that is not easily fixed), `major` (a proof step is wrong or an assumption is missing/misstated, but the result is likely fixable), `minor` (imprecision, notation, missing case that does not change results), or `nit`.
- **Location**: section and result number.
- **Finding**: what is wrong, stated precisely.
- **Evidence**: a counterexample (explicit DAG / distribution / numbers) or the exact faulty step.
- **Suggested fix**, if you have one.

Order findings by severity. If you verify a result and find no problem, say so briefly in a final "checked and found sound" list, so we know what was covered. Do not rewrite the report.

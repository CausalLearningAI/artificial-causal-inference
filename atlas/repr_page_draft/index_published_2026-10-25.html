Interpretable Representation × Behavioral Sciences

One label-free recipe that turns any behavioural video into about 1,500 readable numbers.

Riccardo Cadei · 2026-10-25 · describes the frozen model v2

Objectives

What we want.

  1. Readable. Each dimension is a neuron you can look at and name.
  2. General. One recipe for any species and lab setup.
  3. Usable. Each video becomes a fixed-length vector for downstream analysis.

Principles

How we build it.

Label-free
Labels never shape the representation. They only score it.
Simple
As few components as possible. Each one encodes a stated prior.
Explicit
Every prior is written down and tagged where it acts.

Evaluation protocol

Two scores, both computed after training. Labels and treatment never shape the representation; labels only score it.

a · Labelled alignment

  • Primary: AUROC of the best single neuron, cross-fitted: picked on half of the videos (or pools), scored on the other half. A domain's primary AUROC is the mean over its behaviours.
  • Secondary: nAP (average precision, rescaled so 0 = chance, 1 = perfect) and best-threshold F1 per frame.
  • Why AUROC. The pre-registered metric audit: validity tied (AUROC 0.389, nAP 0.388, R̃ 0.386, F1 0.386), so the most reliable won (split-half reliability AUROC 0.56 vs R̃ 0.23).
  • Its weakness. AUROC rewards broad neurons: a “two mice touching” neuron ranks MABe22 sniffing at AUROC 0.84 with AP 0.017. So nAP is always reported next to it.
  • Video-level r and bout F1 were poor neuron selectors in the audit and are not used to rank.
Legacy score R̃Used in older tables on this page; kept as a descriptor only.›
R~b=Pb−πb1−πb
  • Pb (R-precision): rank frames by the best neuron, take as many top frames as there are frames of behaviour b; Pb is the share that show b. πb: the share of frames with b. 0 = chance, 1 = perfect.
  • Mice primary R̃ = mean of nose-nose and nose-tail R̃ on mice v1. Older recipe decisions required the domain-averaged R̃ to rise by ≥ 0.01 over 6 training runs (95% interval above 0).

b · Label-free capacity

Capacity = the number of distinct behaviour neurons: neurons that pass all eight checks, with near-duplicates merged (correlation ≥ 0.5 or firing overlap ≥ 0.5). The checks never see labels or treatment.

Not background
At most half of its firing frames peak on a token with < 5% animal pixels.
Not static
At most half of its variance lies between videos (lighting, bedding, session).
Bout-like
Its memory (autocorrelation time) lies between 0.2 s and 120 s.
Not tied to conditions
Recording day, batch, position or camera period explain at most half of its per-video mean (cross-validated R², 72–192 videos).
Reproducible
A dictionary retrained with another seed has a neuron correlated ≥ 0.5 with it.
Invariant
Correlation ≥ 0.5 with itself after rotating, mirroring or brightening the frame by ±10%.
Position-free
Where it fires matches where animals go (distance ≤ 0.5 on an 8 × 8 grid). Catches place neurons: mice n1802 at the spout (0.80), ants n8 at a wall marker (0.72).
Not a body part
On in at most half of the frames with animals. Weak: 95–100% of neurons pass.

  • In mice the checks do not pick out labelled neurons (enrichment AUROC 0.517 [0.495, 0.539]); in ants they do (0.654 [0.638, 0.669]).
  • Capacity mostly scales with dictionary width: the first 256 neurons hold only 19 of 131 (mice) and 24 of 79 (ants) behaviour neurons.

c · Readout

A per-neuron 3-state hidden Markov model, fit without labels, gives P(on) per frame. It keeps the ranking (nAP +0.002 vs continuous values). In ants it is calibrated to the behaviour (frame F1 0.59–0.88, Brier skill > 0); in mice it reflects the neuron's own state, on ≈10× the behaviour's time (on 4–21% of frames vs 1–2% labelled).

Search
  • Discoveries come with confounds. Neural effect search on v2 finds many neurons only where the compared groups also differ in recording conditions: ants v4 infested vs healthy colony (also a different colony, recorded on different days; up to 13 neurons on bout rate), ants v3 t=2 vs t=8 (t=8 recorded only on one day, in a brighter arena; up to 17), and tadpoles En1 vs wild type (each recording session holds one group only; up to 18). Contrasts without such a confound (ants v2 t=1 vs 2, v3 t=2 vs 6) give 0 to 1 neuron.
  • The one annotated effect is missed. In the labels, heterozygous mice spend more time nose-to-nose from phase 4 to 5 (+0.53% of time, paired over 18 annotated pools, p 0.003). No model (v0, v1 or v2) recovers it.
  • Stability. Each selected neuron is re-tested under alternative windows, levels and bout rules; on mice, 28 of the 99 selected neurons survive every check (step d).

aVideoCamera resolution, never enlarged, in a canonical orientation, 5 fps.Same layoutSufficient resolution›

Resolution. Each video enters at the camera's own resolution, never upsampled, and is cut into 16 px patches. The target is at least about 7 patches per body length where the camera allows. Mice: 1024 px, down from the camera's 2064 px (9.4 patches per body; 2048 px gave no clear gain). Ants and frogs: native size (ants reach only 4–5 patches per body). Tadpoles are only 2.4 patches long at camera resolution, so a 96 px crop that follows the animal is enlarged to 384 px: the one exception to “never enlarge”. Cost grows with resolution squared.

Canonical orientation. Every video is turned so that the experimental layout looks the same: mice with the odour corner at top right, juvenile frogs mirrored so the mutant side is always on the same side.

Whole frame in at the resolution of step a, cut into 16 px patches (mice at 1024 px: a 64 × 64 token grid), read at block 20 of 24: 1024 dimensions per token. Animal maskTight: keep a token only if its 16 px patch is at least 25% animal pixels. No border ring.On the bodiesAnimals stand out›

Animal pixels are found with a simple per-domain cue against the empty arena (for ants: darker than the background by more than 40 grey levels). The mask is computed on the same fine token grid the encoder uses. The mask is applied after the encoder, so kept tokens still carry context from the whole frame.

Why no ring. v1 grew the mask by a one-patch border. In the first finer-grid rollout after v1, that ring was computed on the coarse grid and projected onto the fine one, and in mice 47% of the kept tokens were then background bedding around the animals. Neurons for that ring fired strongly and won the per-frame maximum (step c), so the "best" neurons lit up next to the mice, not on them.

Sparse dictionaryOne dictionary per domain: 2048 neurons, nested 128 / 512 / 2048, 16 active per token on average.Few concepts per patchOne vocabulary per setup›

A sparse autoencoder, trained per domain on animal tokens, no labels. Matryoshka: nested sizes. The first neurons must reconstruct the tokens on their own, so they take the frequent concepts; later neurons add rarer ones. This encodes the long-tail prior, and any prefix is a smaller dictionary. In a plain dictionary, a frequent general concept can split into many specific neurons as the width grows.

Threshold
  • In training, BatchTopK keeps the 16 × N largest activations among the N tokens of a batch. A busy token can use more neurons and a plain one fewer.
  • At inference a token must not depend on its batch-mates, so each activation above one fixed value θ is kept.
  • θ is calibrated once on held-out training tokens so that 16 neurons fire per token on average. This is the same rule applied to the whole training set at once. It replaced a running average; scores move by at most 0.0004.
Why 16
  • k counts concepts per patch, not behaviours. A 16 px patch holds a body part, its pose, texture, contacts and place; behaviour is one or two of the 16.
  • MABe22: k = 8 lowers primary AUROC by 0.049. Lab mice (v1 era): k = 4 / 8 / 32 within noise. Not re-run on v2. What matters for behaviour is how often a neuron fires per frame, after the max over about 77 to 150 animal tokens.

dVideo representation0.6 s centred smoothing, then per neuron and video: mean, bout rate, latency, all from the continuous smoothed values. No on/off state.Short-range in timeEthogram›

Smoothing. Each neuron's frame values are averaged over the frame before, the frame itself and the frame after (0.6 s at 5 fps). This is the only temporal step in v2.

Per-video summaries. Three numbers per neuron per video, all computed from the continuous smoothed values (the default for alignment and for the search): the mean value over the video; the bout rate (bouts per minute above a high level of that neuron, its 95th percentile pooled over videos); and the latency (time to the first frame above that level).

Expected fraction of time active. For readers who want “share of time the neuron is on”, each neuron also has a label-free probability of being on in every frame, P(on), from a per-neuron 3-state hidden Markov model of its raw 5 fps values. Averaged over a video, it gives the expected fraction of time active; secondary outcome, mean only. In mice it tracks the neuron’s state, not the behaviour (Evaluation protocol, c).

Search. NES (neural effect search) and its successor NESS test, neuron by neuron, whether a per-video summary differs between experimental groups (Bonferroni-corrected). They run on the smoothed values directly. Two ways of turning the values into on/off states were tested and both lower alignment, so neither is used. The first, pre-specified, is one firing threshold for all neurons (fires when its scaled value passes a level set so that 16 neurons fire per frame on average). The second fits, per neuron and without labels, two log-normal states (“low” and “high”) to the logarithm of its smoothed values and calls the neuron on when the high state is more likely:

Stability
  • Every neuron the search selects in the main setting is re-tested under a set of reasonable alternative settings, and we count how many it survives. Main setting: Bonferroni correction; a Welch t test on the per-video summary (paired t test where the mice contrast compares two phases of the same pool); mice habituation = its last 15 min.
  • Checks for the mean: the window (mice habituation over its whole 30 min; the first 30 s of every window dropped). For bout rate: the level at the 90th or 99th percentile instead of the 95th, merging bouts less than 2 frames apart, and the same two window checks. For latency: the 90th / 99th percentile and the window checks.

Observed limitations

What the pipeline does not do yet, measured on the validation data. Possible fixes are listed under Pending tests.

  1. Two-animal interactions. v2 is much better on mouse nose-nose sniffing (nose-nose R̃ 0.20 vs 0.07 for v1), but nose-tail is still below the deployed v1 (nose-tail R̃ 0.08 vs 0.11; v1’s mean over 11 training runs is 0.057). The information is in the tokens: one supervised direction on the same tokens, read with the same per-frame maximum, reaches nose-nose R̃ 0.35 and nose-tail R̃ 0.33 (AUROC 0.88 / 0.90). The dictionary does not give that direction a neuron of its own: it is a low-variance direction, and reconstruction spends neurons on frequent, high-variance concepts. In MABe22 the picture is the same: a supervised probe reaches AUROC 0.88, our best neuron ranks almost as well (AUROC 0.84) but is not precise (AP 0.017, chance 0.002). It fires wherever two mice touch.
    • Ruled out: video encoders (V-JEPA 2), temporal context channels, pair tokens, invariance and prediction losses, every sparsity variant, 8192 neurons, unions of 20 dictionaries, whitening, ICA, cluster-balanced sampling, resolutions from 512 to 1408 px, spatial context (3 × 3 with 8192 neurons did not hold up on fresh runs), wider animal masks (M1 / M2, step b). Numbers under “Tried and discarded”.
    • Sharper input does not fix it: at 1024 px on CalMS21, the best face-sniffing neuron reaches normalised R-precision 0.13 (0.18 without the change channel).
  2. The original recipe (v0) does not find behaviour. We reran the original ECI code unchanged on ants v2; it reproduces our reimplementation (same tokens, same training curve). Its hits lean towards grooming (with the mean-pooled input, 8 of 10 hits are in the top 13% of neurons by grooming AUROC), but the alignment is weak (AUROC 0.52–0.59), the hits change with every training run, and nothing survives correction for multiple testing (Bonferroni or false discovery rate) across 36 runs.
Known limits
  • No tracking. A neuron does not say which animal acts, or on whom; reliable tracking is missing for most videos. Two copies of a behaviour in one frame count once (0.5% of nose-nose frames in public mouse data; up to 7% of frames in our ants).
  • Frame rate. 5 fps was checked only on per-video totals; about half of the nose-nose contacts in our mice span a single frame.
  • Transfer. The public data has only mice and one beetle, and the earlier recipe scored lower on our mice. So v2 was chosen on our own labels (never inside training).
